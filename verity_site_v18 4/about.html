About & methodology

What Verity actually measures, what it doesn't, and where your data goes.

What this is

Verity is a demo screening tool. It runs two short tests in your browser — a spoken picture description and a handwritten sentence copy — and computes simple timing/acoustic/motion markers from what you say and write. Those markers are compared against patterns that published research has associated with cognitive aging, and turned into a rough "pattern-match" percentage and band (typical / some markers / several markers).

What we measure

Speech

Recording length, estimated speaking rate, time spent speaking vs. silent, number of pause segments, and vocal energy variability — all from acoustic timing, not a transcript.

Handwriting

Total writing time, stroke (pen-lift) count, average and variability of writing speed, and mid-stroke hesitations — captured from your pointer/touch input.

Your data & privacy

Everything runs in your browser. Audio, handwriting strokes, and results are processed locally and never uploaded anywhere. Your account and screening history are saved to this browser's local storage only.

Passwords are salted and hashed (SHA-256 where your browser supports it) before being stored — never saved in plain text.

From your dashboard you can export all your data as a JSON file, print a doctor-ready summary, or permanently delete your account and history.

Non-drug approaches worth knowing about

Verity doesn't provide treatment — but if a result (or a general concern) has you thinking about what actually helps, these are approaches with real research behind them. Discuss any of these with a doctor before starting, especially alongside an existing diagnosis or medication.

Cognitive Stimulation Therapy (CST)

A structured program of group activities and discussion designed to actively engage thinking, memory, and social interaction, typically run twice a week over several weeks. It's one of the few non-drug interventions for mild-to-moderate dementia backed by randomized controlled trials, and it's the only non-drug treatment the UK's NICE guidelines recommend for that stage of dementia. Usually delivered by a trained facilitator — ask a doctor or memory clinic about local or online programs.

Physical exercise

Regular aerobic exercise and strength training are consistently linked to better cognitive health and may help preserve brain structure and function over time. It's one of the more strongly evidenced lifestyle factors across the research.

Social engagement

Staying socially connected — regular conversation, group activities, community involvement — is repeatedly associated with slower cognitive decline. It's also a core ingredient of CST itself.

Diet (Mediterranean / MIND-style patterns)

Diets emphasizing vegetables, fish, olive oil, nuts, and whole grains, with less red meat and processed food, show promising associations with cognitive health, likely related to their anti-inflammatory effects.

Sleep & vascular health

Managing blood pressure, treating hearing loss, and prioritizing good sleep are increasingly recognized as modifiable dementia risk factors — addressing them is a reasonable, low-risk step regardless of test results.

Multidomain programs

Combining several of the above — cognitive training, exercise, diet, and social activity together — appears to have additive benefits compared with any single approach alone, according to recent multidomain intervention trials.

Sources: CST narrative review, NCBI · Non-pharmacological therapies network meta-analysis, Frontiers · Multidomain dementia prevention review, PMC · NIH 2025 Alzheimer's research progress report
